# Voice guide: Dana Whitfield

Built from 4 pasted samples (two LinkedIn posts, a customer email, a bio) on
2026-08-22.

## Rules

1. **Open with a scene from a plant floor, not a thesis.**
   Example: "Watched a validation engineer print 340 pages on Tuesday. For one line change."
2. **Exact numbers always.** 340 pages, 12 years, 30 plants. Vague quantities are someone else talking.
   Example: "Our last audit prep took 4 days. It used to take 6 weeks."
3. **Short declarative sentences. One idea each.**
   Example: "The binder is not the evidence. The binder is where evidence goes to die."
4. **Dry, deadpan asides. Never flagged as jokes.**
   Example: "I spent 12 years validating things. Mostly my own patience."
5. **Blunt about the industry, warm to individuals.**
   Example: "Quality engineers are the most underrated people in medtech. Their tooling is the most overrated."
6. **Authority from the work, never the title.** References running protocols by hand, audit weeks, plant installs.
   Example: "I've signed more deviation reports than business cards."
7. **End on a verdict or a plain question a buyer would actually answer.**
   Example: "If your audit prep takes six weeks, that's a design choice. Someone else's."
8. **No hashtag walls.** Zero or one hashtag.
9. **Plain formatting.** Short paragraphs, no emoji, no bullet-list posts.
10. **Technical terms used straight, then translated once.**
    Example: "IQ/OQ/PQ. In English: prove it's installed right, runs right, and keeps running right."

## Never do

Corporate buzzwords: "leverage", "utilize", "harness", "optimize",
"streamline", "synergy", "revolutionize", "transform", "elevate", "empower",
"spearhead", "foster", "cultivate", "delve", "landscape", "paradigm",
"holistic", "robust", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "game-changer",
"innovative", "unlock".

Essay transitions: "In conclusion", "To summarize", "Furthermore",
"Moreover", "Additionally", "Consequently", "That being said", "It goes
without saying".

Filler openers: "In today's [anything]", "As we move forward", "It's no
secret that", "In the ever evolving world of", "It's worth noting that",
"Needless to say", "In this day and age", or any opener that could start
anyone's post.

AI/marketing fluff: "Excited to share", "Thrilled to announce", "I'm
humbled", "It's been an incredible journey", "I'm passionate about", "Let's
dive in", "Without further ado".

Structural tells: em dashes (use a period, comma, or parentheses instead);
rule-of-three padding ("faster, smarter, better") standing in for a real
point; two or more consecutive sentences starting with the same word; hollow
engagement-bait closers ("Thoughts?", "Who else? Let me know below!").

Founder's own list: "best-in-class", "compliance journey", "quality culture"
(she says it's what plants say instead of fixing things).
